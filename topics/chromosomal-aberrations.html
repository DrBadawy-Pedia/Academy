01

Causes & Numerical Anomalies

Risk factors · non-disjunction · trisomy & monosomy · mosaicism

Causes

  • Maternal age >35 years → ↑ non-disjunction
  • Genetic predisposition (Fanconi anemia, Ataxia telangiectasia)
  • Exposure to radiation, drugs, viruses, autoimmune disorders

Non-disjunction

Failure of separation of two homologous chromosomes → 2 daughter cells: one with an extra chromosome, one missing a chromosome.

Chromosomal vs Chromatid Non-disjunction

ChromosomalOccurs during meiosis → single abnormal cell line (monosomy lethal except Turner; trisomy)
ChromatidOccurs during mitosis → mosaic pattern, milder, 2–3 cell lines (e.g. mosaic Turner, mosaic Down)
Numerical Anomaly Outcomes Meiotic non-disjunction→Trisomy (1 cell line)orMonosomy (usually lethal, except Turner)
Mitotic (chromatid) non-disjunction→Mosaic — 2 or 3 cell lines, milder phenotype
02

Structural Chromosomal Anomalies

Deletion · ring · inversion · isochromosome · fragile site · translocation

Deletion (del)

A part of a chromosome is broken & lost.

  • Deletion 5p → Cri du Chat: microcephaly, MR, cat-like cry (laryngeal hypoplasia), congenital heart disease
  • Deletion Xp → Turner syndrome

Ring Chromosome (r)

Two breaks in the peripheral parts of a chromosome, then the ends stick together → non-functioning chromosome.

Example: Ring X chromosome (Turner syndrome)

Inversion (inv)

Two breaks in a chromosome with inversion of the intervening part.

Example: Cat Eye syndrome

PericentricParacentric
Occurs in both arms, around centromereOccurs in same arm, away from centromere

Isochromosome (i)

Transverse division of a chromosome during cell division → 2 identical long arms (2q) + 2 identical short arms (2p).

Example: Isochromosome Xq (Turner syndrome)

Fragile Chromosome

After culture on folate-deficient media, the chromosome shows elongation at one point (fragile site) → non-active chromosome.

Example: Fragile X chromosome (Fragile X syndrome)

Translocation (t)

A part of a chromosome is broken & joined to another chromosome.

Balanced (BTC)Same cell, no loss of genetic material
UnbalancedOccurs during gametogenesis
A — Reciprocal TranslocationB — Robertsonian Translocation
MechanismExchange of broken (unequal) parts between two non-homologous chromosomes2 acrocentric chromosomes (Group D & G), 2 breaks near centromeres, unequal exchange → one big (2q) + one short lost chromosome (2p)
Examplet 9/22 (Philadelphia chromosome)Translocation Down

Insertion: a broken part is inserted into another chromosome.
