01

Autosomal Inheritance

Autosomal Dominant vs Autosomal Recessive

Autosomal Dominant (AD)

  • Genes on autosomes → affect both sexes equally
  • Manifests whether homozygous or heterozygous (no carrier state)
  • Any affected person has an affected parent
  • Trait appears in all generations

Autosomal Recessive (AR)

  • Manifests only in homozygous state
  • Carrier = heterozygous state
  • Both parents are carriers
  • Consanguinity may be present

AD Examples

Hereditary SpherocytosisVon Willebrand DiseaseNeurofibromatosisTuberous SclerosisAchondroplasiaOsteogenesis ImperfectaMarfan SyndromeEhlers-DanlosCrigler-Najjar IIGilbert Disease

AR Examples

ThalassemiaFanconi AnemiaSickle Cell AnemiaGalactosemiaPhenylketonuriaAlbinismCrigler-Najjar I

AD Cross: Affected (Dd) × Unaffected (dd) Dd × dd→50% Dd (affected)+50% dd (unaffected)
Each pregnancy: 50% affected
AR Cross: Carrier (Rr) × Carrier (Rr) Rr × Rr→25% RR (free)+50% Rr (carrier)+25% rr (affected)
Each pregnancy: 75% unaffected, 25% affected
02

Y-linked & X-linked Inheritance

Y-linked · X-linked recessive · X-linked dominant

Y-linked Inheritance

  • Rare
  • Only males are affected
  • Only male-to-male transmission
  • Related to male sex determination / reproduction

Male infertilityHairy pinna (Hypertrichosis of ears)Webbed toes

X-linked Recessive (XR)

AffectsMales (females may be carriers, XX)
ManifestsOnly in homozygous state
Affected fatherAll sons free, all daughters carriers
Carrier mother50% sons affected, 50% daughters carriers

G6PD deficiency (Favism)Hemophilia A, B

X-linked Dominant (XD)

AffectsBoth males & females (dominant)
ManifestsHomozygous or heterozygous (no carrier)
Affected fatherAll sons free, all daughters affected
Affected mother50% sons and 50% daughters affected

Familial Hypophosphatemic RicketsRett Syndrome

When Do XR Diseases Affect Females?

  • Homozygous female XX (affected father + carrier mother)
  • Turner syndrome XO (only one X)
  • Lyon theory: skewed X-inactivation may give mild effect in a heterozygous female
  • New gene mutation
XR Cross: Normal Father × Carrier Mother XY × XX(carrier)→50% daughters carriers+50% sons affected
03

Non-Mendelian Inheritance

Multifactorial · Mitochondrial · Genomic imprinting

Multifactorial

Genes + environmental factors.

  • CNS anomalies
  • Cleft lip & palate
  • Congenital heart diseases
  • Cancers
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Hypertension

Mitochondrial

Mother transmits it to both sexes (maternal inheritance).

  • MELAS
  • MERRF
  • Rye syndrome

Genomic Imprinting

Expression depends on parental origin — the gene is inactivated in one gamete.

  • Prader-Willi syndrome (paternal imprinting)
  • Angelman syndrome (maternal imprinting)
04

Karyotyping Basics

Definition · source of cells · banding techniques · chromosome shapes

Definition

KaryotypingAnalysis & photographing of chromosomes by special techniques for diagnostic purposes, done during cell division, especially metaphase.

Source of Cells

  • Blood lymphocytes: routine, needs 6 days
  • Bone marrow cells: leukemia, rapid (6 hours)
  • Buccal mucosa: for sex chromatin (Barr body)
  • Skin fibroblasts: mosaicism, post-mortem, crimes
  • Samples from cancers or masses
  • Gonads (testis or ovary)
  • Chorionic villi sample & amniotic fluid cells: prenatal diagnosis

Banding Techniques

G bandingGeimsa stain, light microscope, appears during metaphase
High-res bandingGeimsa, electron microscope, prophase
Q bandingQuinacrine, fluorescent microscope
R bandingReverse of G banding
C bandingCentromeric banding, for counting only

Classification by Centromere Site

MetacentricCentromere central, p ≈ q
SubmetacentricCentromere near center, p < q
AcrocentricCentromere close to end; very short p arm (no genes), very long q arm; may carry a satellite
Denver Classification (by size & centromere site) Group A: pairs 1,2,3 — large, metacentric·Group B: pairs 4,5 — large, submetacentric
Group C: 6→12 + X — medium, submetacentric·Group D: 13,14,15 — medium, acrocentric
Group E: 16,17,18 — small, submetacentric·Group F: 19,20 — small, metacentric
Group G: 21,22 + Y — small, acrocentric

p = petite (short arm) · q = the next letter after p (long arm)

05

Medical Indications for Karyotyping

When to order a karyotype · Barr body · fragility tests

Clinical Indications

  • Ambiguous genitalia at birth (medical emergency, especially CAH)
  • Amniocentesis for mother >35 yrs or previous diseased child
  • Parents with ≥2 abortions of unknown cause (75% due to chromosomal disease)
  • To confirm a clinical diagnosis
  • Cancer: leukemia, lymphoma, neuroblastoma
  • Congenital deformities / dysmorphic features (face, eyes, ears, mouth, limbs)
  • Delayed puberty of unknown cause (Turner ♀ – Klinefelter ♂)
  • Short stature / FTT of unknown cause (Turner – Down)
  • Mental retardation of unknown cause (Down – Klinefelter – Fragile X)

Barr Body Rule

FormulaBarr bodies = number of X chromosomes − 1
NegativeNormal male, Turner female
PositiveNormal female, Klinefelter male

Special Tests

  • After folate depletion → chromosomal fragility (Fragile X)
  • After mutagen exposure → chromosomal breakage (Fanconi anemia)
