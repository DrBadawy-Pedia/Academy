01

Personal History

Begin with identification and contact information.

Write the personal history in one sentence.

Identity & context

  • Full name, age and sex.
  • Residence and order in the family.
  • Socioeconomic level.

Contact details

  • Date.
  • Telephone number or email for contacting the patient.
02

Complaint

Record the concern in the patient or parent's words.

  • Take it from the parents, or from the child if old enough.
  • Avoid leading questions. Ask: “What’s brought you in today?”
  • Use no scientific terms in the complaint.
  • When there is more than one complaint, write them by importance or in chronological order.
  • Write the complaint in one sentence.
Example from the sourceThe parent’s complaint of elevation of body temperature followed by vomiting and loose watery motions 2 days back.
03

Present History

Analyze the complaint, the same system, then other systems.

  1. Analyze each complaint in a sentence.
  2. Review other symptoms of the same system, including relevant positives and negatives.
  3. Review other systems, reporting positives and useful negatives.

1. Analysis of the complaint

Describe when the condition started, its onset, course and duration (for example, one week ago).

Onset

  • Sudden: within seconds to minutes.
  • Acute: within hours to a few days.
  • Gradual: within 1–2 weeks.
  • Insidious: within weeks and months.

Course

  • Progressive.
  • Regressive.
  • Stationary.
  • Intermittent.
  • For the complaints, mention the scientific term, character and severity, if present.
  • State associated symptoms and what increases or decreases the complaint.
  • If there is more than one complaint, discuss each one separately.

2. Other symptoms of the same system

Answer all relevant negative and/or positive history points. Good negative history helps to:

  • Rule in or rule out involvement of a system.
  • Identify the nature and etiology of the disease process.
  • Assess severity and complications.

3. Symptoms of other systems

Write positives and good negatives only, if present.

If none are associated“No symptoms suggestive for other system affections.”
04

Past History

Perinatal events, prior disease, immunization, development and diet.

Perinatal history: discuss in detail for infants under 2 years and when the case is related to a birth problem.

Antenatal period

Illnesses, infection (TORCH), iatrogenic factors (drugs) and irradiation.

Birth (natal)

Gestational age, birth weight, delivery complications, place and mode of delivery.

Neonatal period

Illness, jaundice or any discoloration, and NICU admission.

Other past events

  • Medical illnesses, allergies and current medication.
  • Major surgical illnesses: operations and dates.
  • Trauma: fractures and lacerations.
  • Previous hospital admissions with dates and diagnosis.

Other essential history

  • Immunization: be specific; do not write only “up to date.”
  • Developmental history.
  • Dietetic history: type of food (formula, breast milk, ± solids); whether the child is satisfied after breastfeeding; intake and concentration of artificial feed (for example, ounces of milk); feeding frequency (increased or decreased); special dietary requirements, such as coeliac disease or lactose-free formula.
05

Family & Social History

Similar conditions, pedigree, environment and day-to-day context.

Family history

  • A similar condition may reflect shared environmental exposure (example in the source: rheumatic heart disease) or genetic cause (example: cystic fibrosis).
  • Draw a family tree or pedigree when considering genetic disease.

Social history

  • Living situation: place, family size, day care and safety issues.
  • Parents' occupations and resources.
  • Schooling and stage of learning.
  • Secondhand smoke exposure: a risk factor for otitis media and bronchial asthma.
