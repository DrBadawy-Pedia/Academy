Diabetes Mellitus Basics

Definition · insulin actions · classification

Definition

DMA disease in which the body's ability to produce or respond to insulin is impaired, causing abnormal carbohydrate metabolism and hyperglycaemia.

Insulin Actions

Glucose↓ blood glucose by ↑ cellular glucose uptake, ↑ gluconeogenesis and ↓ glycogenolysis.
Fat↑ lipogenesis
OverallAnabolic effect

Classification

Type 1 autoimmune β-cell destruction

Type 2 insulin resistance → later β-cell failure

Type 3 other specific types

Type 4 gestational DM

T1DM vs T2DM Clues

  • Multiple autoantibodies
  • Low insulin C-peptide
  • Family history of autoimmune disease
  • Normal BMI

Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

Epidemiology · risk factors · pathogenesis · diagnosis

Epidemiology

  • Peak incidence: fall and winter
  • Common diagnosis: 5–7 years and adolescence
  • Increasing in children <5 years
  • No gender predilection

Risk Factors

  • High-risk alleles
  • First-degree relative / identical twins
  • Congenital rubella, enterovirus or mumps
  • Other autoimmune diseases

Diagnosis

Random≥200 mg/dL + manifestations
Fasting≥126 mg/dL
2-hour OGTT≥200 mg/dL
HbA1c≥6.5

Clinical Presentation

PolyuriaPolydipsiaPolyphagia

DKA · secondary nocturnal enuresis · abdominal pain · weight loss · fatigue · recurrent infections.

↓ Insulin→↓ Lipogenesis→↑ FFA→↑ Ketone bodies
Hyperglycaemia→Osmotic diuresis→Polyuria + Polydipsia

Diabetic Ketoacidosis

Recognition · severity · clinical picture · complications
DKA Diagnostic Snapshot
pH < 7.3Glucose > 200 mg/dLHCO₃ < 15 mEq/LKetonemia / KetonuriaDehydration

Severity by pH

Mild: pH 7.2–7.3
Moderate: pH 7.1–7.2
Severe: pH <7.1

Major Danger

Cerebral oedema is listed as the most common cause of death and may be worsened by overhydration and rapid correction of hyperglycaemia.

Clinical Picture

  • Abdominal pain + vomiting
  • Shortness of breath
  • Confusion / stupor / coma
  • Tachycardia + tachypnoea
  • Poor skin turgor + delayed CRT
  • Sweet/musty ketotic breath

Investigations

  • Hyponatremia in advanced dehydration
  • Normal or elevated serum K+
  • Positive serum/urine ketones
  • Low insulin and C-peptide
  • CT brain if cerebral oedema suspected
DKA Pathogenesis Acute insulin insufficiency→Severe hyperglycaemia→Osmotic diuresis→Dehydration
Acute insulin insufficiency→↑ FFA→↑ Ketones→Metabolic acidosis
04

DKA Management Algorithm

Acidotic phase → monitoring → insulin → transition
Overview Assess child→Shock?→0.9% saline 10–20 mL/kg over 1–2 h
Fluid replacement + maintenance→Monitor electrolytes & neurological state→Start insulin after 1 h
Glucose 250–300?→Add 5% glucose to saline 1:1→Transition when improved

Fluids

  • Deficit: 50–100 mL/kg + maintenance + K as needed over next 23–48 h
  • Avoid rapid rehydration
  • Add dextrose when glucose reaches 250–300 mg/dL
  • Bicarbonate usually not indicated

Insulin

  • Infusion 0.05–0.1 U/kg/h after 1 h
  • No bolus
  • Check glucose hourly
  • Gradual fall about 50 mg/h
  • Do not stop IV insulin until 1 h after SC insulin

Monitoring

  • Input & output
  • Na, K, Ca + acid–base status
  • Neurological state
  • Consider central line/catheter if shocked

Shift to SC Insulin

When clinically improved (no vomiting) and acidosis improves:

pH > 7.3
HCO₃ > 16 mEq/L
Stable Na 135–145 mEq/L
05

Lifelong Management & Special Topics

Insulin · monitoring · honeymoon · Somogyi · dawn · MODY

Insulin Regimen

Basal long-acting insulin + pre-prandial rapid/short-acting insulin. Basal is about 40–50% of total daily dose.

Monitoring

Daily blood glucose at least 4 times. HbA1c reflects average glucose over previous 2–3 months; the source states a target below 7% for many patients.

Somogyi vs Dawn

Somogyi: late nocturnal hypoglycaemia → counter-regulatory hormones → early morning hyperglycaemia.

Dawn: overnight growth hormone secretion antagonises insulin → early morning hyperglycaemia.

MODY

Most common monogenic diabetes. Suspect with diagnosis before 25 years, AD inheritance across generations, usually non-obese, and negative islet antibodies.
