Biochemical Investigations

Biochemical

  • Serum calcium is normal, but may be low (normal: 8.8-10.8 mg/dL; 2.2-2.7 mmol/L).
  • Serum phosphate is low (normal: 4.5-6.5 mg/dL or 1.45-2.1 mmol/L).
  • Serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP):
    • High (normal value = 5-25 king Armstrong units or 50-150 unit/l)
    • The most sensitive indicator of rachitic activity?
  • Parathyroid hormone → high.
  • Low Vitamin D levels by measuring serum 25-OH vitamin D, which is the most accurate measure of stores of vitamin D in the body.
< 20 ng/mLDeficiency
20–29 ng/mLInsufficient
30–100 ng/mLNormal

Calcium in Vitamin D Deficiency Rickets

What is the condition of Ca in vitamin D deficiency rickets?

→ Normalized

Low 1,25 (OH)2 D3

  • Hypocalcemia due to decreased Ca absorption
  • Secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Calcium & ph. mobilization from bones
  • Serum calcium is normalized and ph. loss in urine (hypophosphatemia)
Hypocalcaemia (with tetany) may occur with:

Failure of 2ry hyperparathyroidism to occur.
In advanced cases with depletion of bone calcium.
Shock therapy → ↑↑ deposition of calcium /Ph. in bone on the expense of serum calcium

03

Radiological Investigations

By X-ray on the wrist?

Active rickets:

  • Lower end of long bone showing
    • Triad of widening, cupping, fraying
    • Plus → wide joint space
  • The shaft may show:
    • Rarefaction (↓↓ bone density)
    • Green stick fracture
    • Deformity
  • Delayed bone age

Healing rickets

  • The lower ends of long bone showed:
    • Widening
    • Cupping
    • Wide joint space
    • No fraying

Healed rickets

  • The lower ends of long bone showed:
    • No widening or cupping or fraying
    • Normal joint space
    • Straight prominent lower end
04

Treatment

Prevention:

  • Advice exposure to sunlight and vitamin D rich diet and avoid rachitogenic diet.
  • Vitamin D supplement for:
    • For full term: 400 IU/day since birth.
    • For premature: 1000 IU/day since birth.

Curative:

  • Vitamin D3: oral vitamin D for at least 2 months
    • 1,000 to 5,000 IU for 1- to 12-months old infants
    • 5,000 IU for patients over 1 year of age
  • Adequate Ca supply during treatment.
  • Treatment of the cause if present
  • Treatment of complications:
    • Treatment of respiratory or GIT infection
    • Tetany: calcium gluconate 10% slowly IV
    • Deformities: osteotomy and reconstruction if severe and persistent.

Criteria of Improvement

Radiological improvements:

  • Earliest one to improve (after 2 weeks from start of TTT).
  • By appearance of the line of preparatory calcifications.

Biochemical:

  • Earliest biochemical improvement of phosphate.
  • Complete improvements of ALP occurs at the end of racketic process.

Clinical improvements:

  • Of the appetite, muscle tone, …

Skeletal improvements:

  • Take long times and some of these deformities may remain for life

Renal Rickets & Renal Osteodystrophy

Renal Rickets

It is vitamin d resistant type which occur due to:
Renal osteodystrophy or 1 alpha hydroxylase deficiency or Renal tubular defects

Renal osteodystrophy (Renal glomerular Rickets)

  • Pathophysiology: Due to CRF → Ph retention and low 1alpha hydroxylase → hypo Ca → ↑↑ PTH → bone resorption to increase Ca with more ↑↑ Ph.
  • Clinical picture: of CRF plus > 2 years, more deformities?, tetany is rare?.

Laboratory:

  • Increased urea and creatinine
  • Same as Vit D def rickets but with marked ↓↓ in Ca and marked ↑↑ in PH and PTH.

Radiology:

  • Extensive ricketic finding plus
  • Subperiosteal erosions and may be bone cysts (osteitis fibrosa cystica)

Treatment:

  • TTT of the cause
  • ↓↓ ph intake by low P in the diet and use of phosphate chelator (Ca carbonate or acetate)
  • Ca intake in high dose
  • Active vitamin D (one alpha) or activated vitamin D analogue (paricalcitol)
07

Renal Tubular Rickets

Pathophysiology:

  • Phosphaturia → ↓↓ serum phosphate → serum Ca: Ph ratio become inappropriate for mineralization.
  • Metabolic acidosis → ↑↑ bone resorption.

Aetiology and clinical presentation:

Familial hypophosphatemia (XLD)

in which → loss of Ph → rickets plus hypotonia

Fanconi syndromes:

  • Primary: sporadic → rickets plus due to loss of
    • Ph → hypotonia
    • Bicarbonate → acidosis
    • Proteins → muscle weakness
    • May be K → 4 As apathy, atony, abdominal distension and arrhythmia.
    • Glucose due to low renal threshold
  • Hereditary:
    • Cystinosis (Lignac syndrome) (AR) Fanconi like plus
      • Cystine deposition in the liver, spleen, → HSM
      • BM, WBCs, and cornea → detection of cystine crystals by microscopy and slit lamp.
    • Occulo-cerebro renal (Lowe S.) (XLR)
      • Fanconi like plus
      • Cataract and glaucoma
      • Cerebral: MR, hypotonia and hyporeflexia
  • Drug induced (outdated tetracyclines)
Renal tubular acidosis (Lightwood Syndrome)

Fanconi like plus hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis.

Diagnosis of renal tubular rickets

Depends on clinical finding and laboratory data

  • Laboratory:
    • ↓↓ serum Ph with phosphaturia, but normal serum Ca and PTH ↑↑ ALP
    • In 1ry Fanconi: Increased urinary Ph, AA, Bicarbonate, and may be glucose, and K
    • Metabolic acidosis in blood of RTA and 1ry Fanconi.
    • Specific investigations according to the problem
  • Treatment: oral Ph in large doses plus active vitamin D (one alpha).
