Balanced Diet & Caloric Needs

The essential components of adequate nutrition and the age-based caloric supply.

A well-balanced diet should supply

  • Adequate caloric intake.
  • Adequate proportions of elements: 50% carbohydrate, 35% fat and 15% protein.
  • Adequate vitamins and minerals.
  • Adequate amount of water.

Adequate caloric supply

  • First month: 120 cal/kg/day.
  • Rest of the first year: 110 cal/kg/day.
  • Age 1–3 years: 100 cal/kg/day.
  • Then decrease by 10 cal every 3 years until reaching 40 cal/kg/day in adults.
Carbohydrate4 cal/gEnergy supplied by each gram.
Protein4 cal/gEnergy supplied by each gram.
Fat9 cal/gEnergy supplied by each gram.
Balanced proportion

If caloric intake is adequately proportioned as 50% carbohydrate, 35% fat and 15% protein, it is considered a well-balanced diet.

Protein requirements

Essential amino acids

Essential for growth and cannot be synthesized by the body: valine, isoleucine, threonine, arginine, leucine, lysine, tryptophan, methionine, phenylalanine and histidine—plus tyrosine and cystine in children only.

Non-essential amino acids

Can be synthesized by the body but are still needed for growth.

AgeProtein required

Why do children need more protein than adults?

Because of rapid growth and greater liability to gastroenteritis with protein loss.

Fat & Carbohydrate Requirements

Types, functions and the daily carbohydrate need.

Types of fat

  • Saturated fatty acids.
  • Unsaturated fatty acids.
  • Essential fatty acids: long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (LCPUFA), essential for skin integrity, hair growth, CNS development and retinal development.

Functions of fat

  • Support of body structure.
  • Food stores.
  • Temperature preservation.
  • Absorption of fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K.
Daily carbohydrate need

About 10 g/kg/day.

04

Fat-Soluble Vitamins

Vitamin A, E and K: requirements, sources, functions, deficiency and treatment.

Vitamin groups

Water-soluble: vitamin B complex and C; usually not stored, so no toxicity. Fat-soluble: vitamins A, D, E and K; stored in the body, so they may be toxic.

VITAMIN A

Requirements, sources & functions

  • Daily requirement: 1000–2000 IU/day.
  • Retinol, animal sources: liver, meat, fish, egg and milk.
  • Carotene (provitamin), plant sources: dark-green vegetables and carrots.
  • Forms rhodopsin, which is responsible for dark vision.
  • Maintains integrity of mucous membranes and skin.
VITAMIN A DEFICIENCY

Presentation & treatment

  • Eye: night blindness; xerosis of conjunctiva then cornea; keratomalacia with corneal ulceration and damage.
  • Skin: follicular hyperkeratosis.
  • Prophylaxis: oral 200,000 IU every 6 months.
  • Mild symptoms: oral 30,000 IU daily.
  • Severely ill with vomiting: vitamin A palmitate IM.
VITAMIN E · TOCOPHEROL

Core facts

  • Daily requirement: 15–25 IU/day.
  • Sources: breast milk is sufficient; nuts, legumes and green vegetables.
  • Functions: antioxidant and cell-membrane stabilizer.
  • Deficiency causes: prematurity, PEM and malabsorption.
  • Manifestations: hemolytic anemia, edema and thrombocytosis exaggerated by high doses of iron; severe CNS deficiency causes ataxia, weakness and loss of deep-tendon reflexes.
  • Treatment: tocopherol acetate IM for premature infants with decreased polyunsaturated fatty acids in the diet.
VITAMIN K

Core facts

  • Synthesized by intestinal flora; bile salts are needed for absorption.
  • Sources: nuts and leafy vegetables; breast milk is deficient.
  • Essential for synthesis of coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X.
  • Deficiency causes: decreased bacterial flora in newborns or after broad-spectrum antibiotics, malabsorption, and drugs interfering with vitamin action.
  • Manifestation: hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.
  • Prophylaxis: 1 mg natural vitamin K IM immediately after delivery.
  • Treatment: 5 mg natural vitamin K IM daily for 3 doses.

Mineral requirements

CALCIUM

Calcium

Sources: milk and milk products.

  • Bone and teeth formation.
  • Nerve conduction.
  • Muscle contraction.
  • Blood coagulation.
  • Cardiac function.
  • Deficiency causes rickets and tetany.
PHOSPHORUS

Phosphorus

Sources: milk, milk products and meat.

  • Bone and teeth.
  • Structure of muscles.
  • Carbohydrate and fat metabolism.
  • Deficiency causes rickets.
MAGNESIUM

Magnesium

Sources: milk, meat, vegetables and legumes.

  • Bone and teeth.
  • Activation of PTH.
  • Nerve conduction.
  • Deficiency causes tetany.
IRON

Iron

Sources: liver, meat, apples, vegetables and legumes.

  • Hemoglobin.
  • Myoglobin.
  • Oxidative enzymes such as catalase and peroxidase.
  • Deficiency causes iron-deficiency anemia.
