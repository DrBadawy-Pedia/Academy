Abnormal Nutrition & PEM

The nutritional-disorder framework and the two source classifications of protein-energy malnutrition.

Under nutrition

Chronic caloric deficiency

Leads to marasmus.

Over nutrition

Chronic caloric excess

Leads to obesity.

Malnutrition

Element deficiency ± excess calories

May lead to rickets, kwashiorkor and iron-deficiency anemia.

Protein-energy malnutrition

Syndromes resulting from caloric and/or protein deficiency.

02

Kwashiorkor: Definition & Etiology

The edematous form of PEM, its typical age and major dietary and non-dietary causes.

Kwashiorkor · edematous PEM · “red baby”

A severe form of acute protein deficiency with normal or increased caloric intake. The Ghanaian term also refers to the illness of the older child when the next baby is born.

Typical age · 6 months to 2 yearsAge of faulty weaningProtein deficiencyEdema is essential

Maternal deprivation

May occur when another baby is born.

Dietary causes

  • Negligence: giving the first child little milk and much carbohydrate food after the second baby is born.
  • Ignorance: giving a diet rich in carbohydrate and low in protein.

Non-dietary causes: infections

  • Prolonged diarrhea: anorexia, vomiting and protein loss in stool.
  • Measles: complicating enterocolitis.
  • Whooping cough: recurrent vomiting.
03

Constant Clinical Features

Findings that should be present in every case of kwashiorkor.

01 · MENTAL CHANGES

Apathy and loss of interest

  • Apathy, lethargy, peevishness, misery and lack of interest.
  • No mental retardation: the changes are reversible with treatment.
  • Causes: maternal deprivation, markedly decreased amino acids affecting neurotransmitters—especially serotonin—and nicotinic-acid deficiency.
02 · MUSCLE WASTING

Low muscle-to-fat ratio

  • Weak, thin, atrophic muscles with decreased MUAC.
  • Subcutaneous fat is preserved, so the muscle/fat ratio is low.
03 · GROWTH RETARDATION

Weight more affected than length

  • Markedly decreased weight: 60–80%; may be masked by edema and excess subcutaneous fat.
  • Length is less affected because kwashiorkor is an acute illness.
  • Muscle wasting and growth retardation are both due to protein deficiency.
04 · EDEMA

No diagnosis without edema

  • Pitting edema begins on the dorsum of hands and feet.
  • It gradually involves arms, thighs, genitalia, back and face.
  • Ascites and pleural effusion are not detected unless complicated by tuberculosis or septic peritonitis.
  • Causes: hypoalbuminemia with low oncotic pressure; salt and water retention from increased production and decreased hepatic destruction of aldosterone and ADH; increased capillary permeability from weak support.
Diagnostic anchor

There is no diagnosis of kwashiorkor without edema.

04

Variable Clinical Features

Hair, skin, liver, gastrointestinal, hematologic and vitamin-deficiency findings.

HAIR

Hair changes

  • Sparse, straight, fine and easily detached.
  • Pale reddish, gray or white in severe cases.
  • Flag sign: often seen with recovery; alternating light and dark bands. Light zones indicate poor nutrition and dark zones good nutrition.
  • Causes: decreased sulfur-containing amino acids, tyrosine with reduced melanin, and copper.
SKIN

Flaking-paint dermatosis

  • Also called crazy-pavement appearance.
  • Sites: irritated areas—back, buttocks, inguinal region, perineum and back of thighs; unlike pellagra, which occurs on sun-exposed areas.
  • Hyperpigmentation followed by hypopigmentation, cracking, fissuring, ulceration ± infection.
  • Causes: deficiencies of vitamin A, niacin, zinc and essential fatty acids; disturbed amino-acid metabolism and adrenocortical function.
LIVER

Hepatomegaly

  • Soft, reversible liver enlargement; not liable to cirrhosis.
  • Fatty infiltration from decreased lipotropic factors and increased fat mobilization from adipose tissue.
  • Nutritional recovery syndrome: further enlargement at initiation of treatment due to glycogen deposition before fat removal while lipotropic factors remain low.
GIT

Gastrointestinal manifestations

  • Anorexia from mental changes and infection.
  • Vomiting due to gastroenteritis.
  • Diarrhea from mucosal atrophy and/or edema and/or infection.
ANEMIA

Three patterns

  • Microcytic hypochromic: iron deficiency.
  • Macrocytic: vitamin B12 and folic-acid deficiency.
  • Normocytic: protein deficiency.
VITAMIN DEFICIENCY

Associated manifestations

  • Vitamin A: keratomalacia, xerophthalmia, blindness and Bitot spots.
  • Vitamin B: stomatitis, glossitis and beri beri—ataxia and heart failure.
  • Vitamin C: scurvy.
  • Vitamin K: bleeding from hypoprothrombinemia.
  • Vitamin D: active rickets does not occur, but atrophic rickets may occur.
  • Niacin: pellagra—dermatitis, dementia and diarrhea.
05

Complications, Differential & Investigations

The 4 H / ABCDEF complications, diagnostic alternatives and laboratory work-up.

Complications of kwashiorkor: 4 H and ABCDEF

The 4 H

  • Hypoglycemia: enzyme deficiency, liver affection, and decreased L-carnitine and muscle alanine.
  • Hypothermia: caloric deficiency.
  • Hypomagnesemia.
  • Hypokalemia: increased by IV fluids and treatment of acidosis.

ABCDEF

  • A: anemic heart failure.
  • B: bleeding due to hypoprothrombinemia, increased capillary fragility or DIC.
  • C: corneal ulceration leading to irreversible keratomalacia and possible blindness from vitamin A deficiency.
  • D: dehydration and electrolyte disturbance.
  • F: frequent infections—gastroenteritis, chest and skin.

Differential diagnosis

  • Other causes of generalized edema: cardiac, renal, hepatic and angioneurotic.
  • Other causes of skin lesions: pellagra, Addison disease and napkin dermatitis.
  • Other causes of protein-energy malnutrition.

Biochemical changes

Plasma proteins & amino acids

  • Low albumin: < 2.5 g/dL.
  • Elevated gamma globulins due to infection.
  • Amino-acid level may be normal or increased.
  • Increased non-essential/essential amino-acid ratio: normal = 2; 2–3 = subclinical kwashiorkor; ≥ 3 = kwashiorkor.

Metabolic changes

  • Decreased cholesterol and increased free fatty acids.
  • Hypoglycemia.
  • Decreased serum copper, zinc, magnesium and iron.
  • Decreased serum vitamin K, B12 and folic acid.
  • Dilutional hyponatremia: water rises more than sodium.
  • Decreased urinary urea and 17-hydroxy steroids due to disturbed adrenocortical function.

Routine investigations

  • Sepsis work-up with CBC—for anemia and leukocytosis—and CRP.
  • Serum electrolytes, blood glucose and arterial blood gases.
What plasma albumin level is listed in kwashiorkor?
Less than 2.5 g/dL.
What does a non-essential/essential amino-acid ratio ≥ 3 suggest?
Kwashiorkor; 2–3 suggests subclinical kwashiorkor.
