Definition & Causes

Tetany in Pediatrics — Definition

Tetany is a syndrome of increased neuromuscular excitability, usually caused by hypocalcemia, particularly a reduction in ionized calcium.

1. Hypocalcemia

  • Vitamin D deficiency/rickets
  • Hypoparathyroidism
  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Severe hyperphosphatemia

2. Hypomagnesemia

3. Alkalosis

  • Respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilation ► ↓ ionized Ca despite normal total Ca.
4. Less commonly:

severe malabsorption, pancreatitis, massive blood transfusion,

02

Clinical Features

Clinical Features

  • Perioral and acral paresthesia
  • Muscle cramps and fasciculations
  • Carpopedal spasm ???
  • Positive Chvostek sign: facial muscle contraction after tapping the facial nerve.
  • Positive Trousseau sign: carpal spasm after inflation of a BP cuff.
  • Laryngospasm → stridor and potentially airway obstruction
  • Seizures
  • In severe cases: prolonged QT interval and cardiac arrhythmias.
03

Investigations

Investigations

  • Serum total and ionized calcium
  • Serum phosphate, Magnesium, ALP, & PTH
  • 25-OH vitamin D
  • Renal function
  • Blood gas if alkalosis is suspected
  • ECG → look for prolonged QTc
04

Treatment

Acute symptomatic tetany:

  • 10% calcium gluconate IV slowly, with ECG monitoring.
  • Correct significant hypomagnesemia, if present.
  • Treat the underlying cause, e.g. vitamin D deficiency or hypoparathyroidism.
